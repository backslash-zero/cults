## Interviews

Why interviews
- Your research question is about collective, vernacular understanding: the everyday, non-expert view next to the scholarly and the legal one (positionality transcript).
- Open-ended questions rather than fixed answers, to capture people's own examples, associations and criteria in their own words (old survey methodology).
- Your expectation: people would mention very different, contradicting things, shaped by media, and would answer with examples and aspects (positionality transcript).

Who and how you found them
- 28 participants. All people you had spoken to before: friends, friends of friends, acquaintances. Some knew the aim of the research (positionality).
- Instagram stories as a second channel.
- 14 male, 12 female, 1 other, 1 redacted.
- Age 26 to 97, median 34; one age missing (check the 97).
- Nationalities: French 7, Ukrainian 3, German 2, Italian 2, Turkish 2, and one each Slovak, Australian/Iranian, Mexican, and others.
- Living mostly in Germany (18, Berlin included), France (6), and one each in Slovakia, the United States, Spain; one redacted.
- Dates: 5 Aug to 10 Sep 2026, in batches (Batch 1: 11, Batch 2: 2, Batch 3: 7, Batch 4: 2, Instagram: 6).

The questions
- Core: "When you hear the word cult, what comes to mind?" and "Thinking about the example or image you described, what makes it a cult for you?"
- Neutral follow-ups if needed: "What makes you say that?", "Which features feel most important?", "What would make it no longer a cult?"
- Semi-structured: prepared questions, flexible follow-ups, interrupting as little as possible (your positionality text).

In person vs Instagram
- 22 in person, 6 as Instagram text answers (a different format: written, not spoken).
- Language: 22 English, 6 French. The 6 French ones are marked as translated.

Recording, transcription, clean-up
- Google Recorder for the audio transcript.
- Claude for clean-up: typo and transcription-slip corrections only, with your approval checkpoint (your notice of AI use).
- Your manual pass against the audio.
- AI also used to batch the transcripts into a consistent format (positionality transcript).
- Size: 12,181 words in total, median 287 per interview (111 to 1,536).

Metadata kept
- Age, gender, nationality, main language, language spoken, place of residence, interview language, method, date.
- Initial exemplars in order of mention (what came to mind first): 26 coded, of which 12 unclear, 5 classic NRM or religious group, 3 named group, 2 metaphorical, 2 mixed, 1 digital, 1 organisation.
- Anonymisation: one participant (ig-06) asked for demographics to be redacted.

## Literature

Why OpenAlex
- An open scholarly database with an API: queryable, reproducible, with citation counts and field and topic labels. (check: your reason may differ)

Search terms, titles only
- Fixed keyword queries: cult, sect, new religious movements, "extreme beliefs", plus "sectarian drifts" as a bridge to MIVILUDES.
- Matching on titles only, except "sectarian drifts" (title and abstract).
- "NRM" not searched alone: acronym collisions with unrelated fields.

Screening and ranking
- Screening: deduplication, document type, field, subfield, topic, title patterns, publication dates.
- Ranking: citations plus presence across queries, stratified by discipline.
- Exclusion lists: in the appendix.

What you ended with
- 68 documents: 33 articles, 23 books, 12 chapters, from 2001 to 2024.
- 57 with usable full text. The 11 missing are an access bias (paywalled articles, out-of-print books).
- Text extracted with Docling, then checked by hand.

## MIVILUDES

Why the state view
- France has a state body for sectarian drifts: an institutional, legal definition next to the lay and scholarly ones.
- It targets harmful behaviour, not beliefs or groups (stated on its own page).
- "Secte" translates to "cult", so a sectarian drift means cult-like harm (footnote you kept in triage).

The two documents
- The 2022 to 2024 activity report: 94 extracted expressions.
- The page "Comment identifier une dérive sectaire ?" (accessed 27 Aug 2026): 14 extracted expressions, plus the 17 criteria.

Criteria and expressions
- 17 criteria copied verbatim in French, with a working English translation (not official).
- 108 extracted expressions in total, shown as a figure in the text (triage item 12).
