Niebuhr added the "denomination" category to describe how sects, over generations, soften and assimilate into mainstream society as their founders' radical children become respectable — a dynamic model rather than a fixed label.

-> Interesting to understand where the frontier lies.

Rodney Stark and William Bainbridge later refined the sect/cult split specifically: sects are schismatic breakaways from an existing religious body (they have a lineage), while cults are syncretic and novel, typically formed around a charismatic leader with no prior institutional tie. This is close to what you're circling: "cult" isn't a content-based judgment about how extreme a belief is, but a structural/relational one about the group's tension with, and legitimacy in the eyes of, surrounding society.

Sentinel Island example. Sociologist Eileen Barker spent years studying the Unification Church ("Moonies") specifically to test the "brainwashing" narrative used to delegitimize new religious movements, and found it explained neither why many attendees never converted nor why so many members voluntarily left — undermining the idea that "cult" beliefs are held through some categorically different, pathological mechanism than "legitimate" religious belief. She and others argue the brainwashing framing functioned less as science and more as justification for coercive "deprogramming" interventions against groups society had already decided were illegitimate.

Your US-vs-Europe observation has a direct legal-theoretical basis. France's laïcité model formally distinguishes "true religions" from sectes ("dangerous cults"), and French law grants the state authority to dissolve organizations classified this way — something structurally impossible under the US Establishment Clause, which treats all religions as formally equal and has no legal category for "cult" at all.

-> why it's interesting to look at a legal apparatus that deals with the concept of cults. Culture Determines Where the Boundary Sits. This is also present in the paper adeliyantousUsingLawLimit2023 page 4:
