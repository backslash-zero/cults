\chapter{Survey Methodology}\label{ch:survey_methodology}

\section{Overview}
This study investigates how people subjectively understand the term \enquote{cult} through a short, in-person qualitative survey/interview format. The design uses open-ended questions rather than fixed-response items in order to capture participants' own examples, associations, and criteria in their own words.

\section{Research Aim}
The aim is to identify the dimensions people use when they describe, recognise, or define a cult.

\section{Ethics and Data Management}
The study follows the Declaration of Helsinki \parencite{wmaDeclarationHelsinki2013}, ensuring informed consent, voluntary participation, risk minimisation, privacy protection, and careful handling of identifiable data.

\section{Design}
Data is collected using a self-built survey tool hosted at \url{https://research.celest.in}. Sessions are conducted in person to support engagement, clarify instructions, and improve response quality. With participant consent, responses are audio-recorded and transcribed for later analysis.

\section{Participants and Data Collected}
A pilot run tests question wording and procedure with a small number of participants (\textit{n}~=~10). The need for further responses is determined based on the diversity of responses obtained and on whether additional interviews continue to produce new dimensions. Basic demographic metadata is collected for each participant: age, gender, language of interview, country of residence, and nationality.

\section{Interview Protocol}\label{sec:interview_protocol}
The core interview consists of two open-ended prompts:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \enquote{When you hear the word \textit{cult}, what comes to mind?}
	\item \enquote{Thinking about the example or image you described, what makes it a cult for you?}
\end{enumerate}
If needed, neutral follow-up prompts may be used to clarify meaning, such as:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \enquote{What makes you say that?}
	\item \enquote{Which features feel most important?}
	\item \enquote{What would make it no longer a cult?}
\end{itemize}
These probes are intended to support elaboration without imposing a predefined framework.

\section{Procedure}
Each participant receives a short verbal explanation of the study before taking part. After consent is obtained, the participant answers the two open-ended prompts in person, along with follow-up questions for clarification where needed. Audio is then transcribed, and transcripts are prepared for analysis in de-identified form.

\section{Analysis}
Responses are reviewed to identify recurring attributes. These attributes are then grouped into candidate dimensions. As a secondary, exploratory step, the same survey prompts may be submitted to multiple large language models in order to compare machine-generated responses against those of human participants.

\section{Outputs}
The outputs of this study are anonymised survey data, interview transcripts where appropriate, and an analysis of the extracted dimensions.
