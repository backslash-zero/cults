\subsection{Legitimacy is legally/culturally constructed}
- quick history of legal apparatus
% GAP CLOSED (dedup): the fuller MIVILUDES history is already written up in

- France's la\"icit\'e model formally distinguishes ``true religions'' from \textit{sectes}; the state can dissolve groups classified as such -- structurally impossible under the US Establishment Clause, which treats all religions as formally equal and has no legal category for ``cult'' at all \autocite[pp.4]{adeliyantousUsingLawLimit2023}.
- Culture determines where the boundary sits: e.g. the US's broader freedom-of-religion doctrine, and its history as a refuge land for religious minorities, extends legitimacy to groups like the Mormons, Scientology, or the Amish in a way other countries don't.
- Legitimacy is socially constructed, not content-based: whether a group counts as a cult is a judgment about its structural/relational tension with, and legitimacy in the eyes of, surrounding society -- not about how extreme its beliefs are \autocite[pp.125]{starkChurchesSectsCults1979}.
